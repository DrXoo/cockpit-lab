# Building the space step by step

A new project starts without a confirmed room, desk, cockpit or accessories. **New project** asks where to start: with the cockpit, which opens the Cockpit editor to choose a base, or with the room, which opens this setup. The first step of the setup also offers **Start with the cockpit** to someone who only designs a cockpit; the room setup then waits at its first step until the Room editor is opened. The first screen of the setup asks for the inside width and depth, in cm. The starting height of 250 cm is shown as an editable reference. The room is not drawn until its dimensions are confirmed.

The room setup has four steps: room, doors and windows, existing furniture and desk. You can go back to the steps you visited and keep the items and their positions. "Add later" records a deferred step; "I don't need a desk" lets you go on without a desk. The setup does not design a cockpit: after it, the room has no cockpit until the project's design is added to it from **Room → Cockpit**, designing one first in the Cockpit editor if there is none. Progress saved when the setup still had a fifth cockpit step opens without it: a project at that step opens with the setup complete, and a cockpit deferred there is no longer pending.

A desk's dimensions are entered before it is added. The monitor and arm, keyboard, PC, mouse and desk mat are turned on one by one. The limits of the current configurator still apply: a 120–200 × 60–80 cm desktop, a height of 66–131 cm and reference legs 66 cm deep. This is not a catalog of real desks.

With the monitor on, the Desk step shows **Monitor position**, as the full editor does, whichever item is selected: the saved arm poses, the automatic positions and a field to save the arm as a new pose.

In step 4, the "Item to move" selector picks the whole desk or any of its active accessories. Selecting from the menu turns dragging on and opens the Desk view for the accessories. They can also be selected and dragged directly in the Desk and Top views; items take priority over the desk mat and the desktop. The panel shows the accessory's local coordinates and its rotation. The mount keeps the constraints of its fixing; "Move monitor and arm" lets you drag ELBOW or VESA and adjust their rotations. Accessories keep their size and stay on the desktop when they fit.

New floor furniture and the desk start in the middle of the room so that the user can arrange them; that position is not presented as a valid solution. Wall shelves start centered on wall AB, with their bottom 140 cm above the floor, as an editable reference. Interference is checked again while dragging or typing coordinates. A project can be finished with warnings, which stay visible.

When you finish, the room editor opens, organized as Room, Furniture, Desk and Cockpit. The cockpit design and its posture are adjusted in the Cockpit editor. On narrow screens you switch between Design, Settings and Checks. The page fills the window; long controls scroll inside their panel.

## Wall shelves

In Furniture you can add a "Wall shelf", as well as the shelves that stand on the floor. You choose the mounting wall, the distance from its starting corner, the width, the depth, the height of the unit and the height of its bottom from the floor. Dragging in the plan moves the shelf along its wall; the selector changes its wall. In the plan, the label marks the wall mounting and the height of the bottom. The 3D view shows the space left underneath.

The checks use the full envelope from the bottom to the top. A piece of furniture can sit underneath if their height ranges do not overlap. The tolerances stay at 0.15 cm in the plan and 0.1 cm in height for interference between furniture and with the setup; envelopes that only touch do not interfere. Resting against the wall at the back is intended. The room bounds and the ceiling are checked with the full installed height. Mounting over a door or window opening is an error if they overlap by more than 0.1 cm both along the wall and in height, even with the window clearance turned off. Shelves entirely above or below a window do not take up its clearance; floor furniture keeps the access clearance as before. Loads, wall strength, fixings and what the shelves hold are not calculated.

The JSON in cm keeps `type: "wallShelf"` in the shared `roomFurniture`, and each layout keeps the shelf's `mountWall`, `offset` and `elevation`. The position and rotation are worked out explicitly from the wall and the dimensions when the project is normalized. The earlier types still stand on the floor and v1 import is kept.

## Built-in wardrobe access

In the Furniture step, "Built-in wardrobe" only adds a front of sliding doors on the wall and a band in front of it that must stay clear. You give the wall, the start from its corner, the total width and the height of the doors from the floor, and the clear space in front. The starting clearance of 80 cm is an editable reference. The starting height matches the ceiling and can be adjusted to the real doors. No inside depth, layout or number of doors is asked for. The doors are drawn as a sliding door symbol, without a swing.

The front is dragged along the wall. The clearance extends into the room and is checked from the floor up to the height of the doors. A shelf entirely above stays out of the front and its clearance. Furniture, the desk, the cockpit or an accessory that reaches more than 0.15 cm into it in the plan and 0.1 cm in height raises an "Access blocked" warning; it is not treated as interference with a wardrobe body. You are also warned if the band leaves the room or is turned off with 0 cm. A continuous way to the wardrobe is not checked, and a layout with warnings can still be saved.

The JSON in cm keeps the front in the shared `roomFeatures` with `type: "wardrobeAccess"`, `wall`, `offset`, `width`, `height` and `clearance`; there is no inside volume. Saved heights are kept when the ceiling changes; if they exceed it, they are fitted to the new ceiling. Accesses without a height take the ceiling's as an editable reference. The menu lists it in Furniture even though its coordinates are relative to a wall. Overlaps with other openings and shelves mounted over the front are detected. Earlier projects still import.

## Model and saving

Whether the desk, the cockpit and each accessory are present is part of the configuration, independent of whether a mesh is visible. Absent items take no part in envelopes, interference, ergonomics, quantities or the summary. The numbers kept for future additions do not mean the item exists.

New projects are saved as JSON **version 4, units `cm`**, which keeps the cockpit design apart from the room (see [layouts](layouts.md#file-format)). Version 2 added item presence and setup progress (`setup`: the current step, the furthest step reached, deferred steps and form drafts). Version 3 stores the project's layouts: `shared` holds every setting the layouts share, with the furniture without its placement, and each entry of `layouts` holds its `id`, `name`, placement `settings`, the placement of every piece of furniture by id (`furniture`) and the furniture it leaves out (`hiddenFurniture`). `activeLayoutId` is the layout that opens. See [layouts](layouts.md). Draft measurements come back when you return, but do not create items until confirmed. This is not the future CAD schema in mm described in `mini-cad.md`. The configurator's cuts are still given in mm and are approximate.

`src/project.ts` adapts v1 files explicitly: it keeps their dimensions and positions, and treats the desk, the cockpit and the accessories that version always created as present. It opens them directly in the full editor. v2 files and designs open as a single layout called "Base". v3 files keep the cockpit design among the room's shared settings; they open with every layout and that design. The storage uses `cockpit-lab-v4`; if it does not exist, `cockpit-lab-v3`, `cockpit-lab-v2` or else `cockpit-lab-v1` is restored without deleting it. A v2, v3 or v4 file resumes its current step. Unknown units or versions are rejected.

A design saved in the browser that can't be read is never overwritten. If it is under `cockpit-lab-v4`, which the next save would replace, it is first copied to `cockpit-lab-unreadable-<date and time>`; if the copy fails, for example because the storage is full, automatic saving stays off for the session. Designs under the older keys are never written, so they stay where they are. A dialog says where the design is kept and offers it for download. This is what `src/project-storage.ts` does on load, and `tests/project-storage.test.mjs` covers each case.

## Verification

`tests/project.test.mjs` checks empty projects, the absence of phantom interference and part lists, a desk without accessories, a cockpit without a desk, restoring progress, the v1 adapter and the rejection of inconsistent data. The earlier tests keep covering the full configurator. The end-to-end tests in `e2e/` cover the full flow, dragging the cockpit and desktop items, saving and resuming.

The collision tolerances are kept: the setup volumes need a vertical and horizontal overlap greater than 0.1 cm; furniture uses 0.15 cm in the plan and 0.1 cm in height. Items intentionally resting on the desktop and on the desk mat are handled separately. Strength and a continuous way in are not calculated.

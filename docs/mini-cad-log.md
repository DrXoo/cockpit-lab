# Cockpit mini CAD: what each slice did

The record of each finished stage and slice of [the plan](mini-cad.md): what was built, where it lives and the decisions made while building it. The plan keeps the goals, the rules and the slices still to come; add a slice's record here when it is done.

## 1. Parts model, catalog and conversion, read only

Done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/`). The conversion puts two 40 corner brackets on every joint, and the feet, peripheral envelopes, bracket fasteners and adjustment travels are estimates from the drawing until real parts are chosen; each says so in its data source. Every base reports the parts its parametric drawing left crossing or unsupported, for stage 2 to fix.

## 2. Profile editor on a design history

Slice 1 was done in November 2026. The project file is v5 and the browser saves it under a new key, `cockpit-lab-v5`; the copy an earlier version saved stays under its own key, untouched, which is how the first save over a v4 project leaves it aside. A project has no cockpit design (`null`) until it gets one, from a cockpit file for now; an empty design is `{ "units": "mm", "history": [] }`, and a history with an operation this version doesn't know is rejected. The room settings that placed the cockpit in each layout, and the monitor position in front of it, were deleted with the parametric cockpit; older files are read without them. The side view and the driver went with the rig drawing, and the cut list export with the cuts; slice 2 brings back the views the editor needs, and slice 6 the parts to buy.

Slice 2 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/history.ts`, `src/shared/undo.ts`). The first kind of operation, `add-anchored-profile`, stores the values of the profile it makes: its name, profile system and section, the axis its length runs along, the axis of its section's long side, its length and its start, the center of the end with the lower coordinate. Each operation has its own identifier (`op-1`) and so does the part it makes (`part-1`); a stored history that repeats one, or holds a value a profile cannot have, is rejected. The project file stays v5: an earlier version rejects a history with this operation as unknown, as planned. A new profile is a 40 × 40, 500 mm long, lying on the floor front to back beside the parts the design has, named after its section and its step (“40 × 40 profile 2”). Lengths go from 10 to 3000 mm and starts stay within 3000 mm of the origin, which keeps mistyped values out of the design. Turning a profile to run along its section's long side turns the section with it, as a quarter turn would. The editor draws every section in any orientation, with one groove per slot on each long face, and shows the selected part in the accent color, with its step pressed in the history. The 3D, top and side views pick the part under a click; a click beside the parts clears the selection. Undo and redo keep the last 100 edits of the design and start again when another project or cockpit file opens. The checks of the design came in slice 6.

Slice 3 was done in November 2026. The `grow-profile` operation stores the part it grows from, the face, the end its distance is measured from (the start is the end with the lower coordinate), the distance, the offset across the face and the way it grows: `"out"`, perpendicular to the face, or a direction along it (`"+z"`), for a profile that lies on the face with its section resting there and runs that way from its place. A profile cannot lie towards the face it lies on; moved to a face across its way, it turns to lie along the part it grows from. Its own values are its name, section, the axis of its section's long side and its length; its position always comes from the part it grows from, so moving or lengthening that part carries it along. Out of an end face it grows from the center of the end and carries the profile on. A stored growth must come after the part it grows from and stay on that part's face: its distance can't be more than the part's length, nor its offset more than half the face. An edit that would leave a growth hanging off, such as shortening its parent too much, is refused with a message that names both parts, until slice 5 brings failed operations. **Grow a profile out of it**, in the inspector, shows the new profile as a preview: a click on a face of the selected part moves it there, measured from the nearer end and centered on the face, where the slot is; the panel sets the same values with numbers.

Slice 4 was done in November 2026. Making a profile a span edits the operation that made it: a `grow-profile` growing out of a face can store `reachesId`, the part whose facing face its free end meets, and its length is then the gap to that part. The part must be in front of the end, on its center line, at its tilt and at a gap a profile can have (10 to 3000 mm), and it cannot hang from the span, directly or through others; a stored span must reach a part of the design that doesn't. After every edit the operation records the length the span takes while it meets its part, so when the faces stop facing each other the span keeps its last length and its end is reported open, in the inspector and in the history, with a ⚠ as well as the color. Its length is not edited while it reaches a part; letting its end go keeps the length it had. A part cannot move so close to a span reaching it that the span would be shorter than a profile. In the A–B–C case, B grows out of A and reaches C, and moving C changes B's length (`tests/cockpit/cad/history/spans.test.ts`).

Slice 5 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/replay.ts`). The history is replayed step by step: an operation whose part is gone at its step (deleted, removed, failed or made at a later step) or that no longer fits there (a growth off its parent's face, a span too short or too long) fails, adds nothing and says why; what depends on it fails too, and the rest applies. Editing an operation is refused only when it would make that same operation fail; editing a part so that the steps hanging from it fail is allowed, and they apply again once it fits them, as do failed steps edited back into place. The `remove-part` operation stores the part it removes: from its step on, the parts that hung from it are anchored where they are, at the tilt of their frame, and spans that reached it keep their length with an open end; its joints go with it. Deleting an operation takes it out of the whole history. A span whose part was deleted keeps its last length with an open end. A part identifier that a remaining operation still refers to is never given to a new part. The history marker shows the design up to a step; new steps go in there and push it on, the steps after it wait, greyed and not selectable, and undo keeps it within the history. It is not saved: a design opens at its last step. Selection is now of steps, so removals and failed steps can be selected and deleted; failed steps are marked with ⚠ and their reason as well as a dashed border.

Slice 6 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/fastening.ts`, `src/cockpit/cad/parts/connectors.ts`). The inspector shows the checks of the whole design, wherever the history marker is: parts reaching into each other, by how many mm; unsupported parts, which are all of them until the design has feet; joints without connectors; and failed steps, with their number and why they fail. Below the checks come the parts to buy: profiles by cut with the names of their parts, connectors, bolts and T-nuts, and feet. Items whose catalog values are estimated say so, and while a joint has no connectors the list warns that it is not complete. The `add-foot` operation puts a leveling foot or a locking caster under a profile: it stores the foot's name, type, profile and its distance from one of the profile's ends, and under an upright it stands under the bottom end. **Add a foot under it** puts one under the end that has fewer, flush with it, so the first two go under both ends. A foot whose place falls off its profile fails, and removing the profile takes its feet with it, as they are fastened to it by their own adapters. Nothing grows out of a foot and no span reaches one. In the editor, a design stands on its lowest part, as it would on a real floor: the floor is drawn under its feet once it has them. The `fasten-joint` operation puts connectors on a joint, the joined part and the part it meets, each with its type and the side of the joined part it sits against; a joint has one such step, which holds all its connectors, and a history that fastens a joint twice is rejected. A corner bracket fits where both its legs rest on their parts: against a side of a part that meets a face end on, and on that face. The inspector of either part lists its joints, with a toggle for each side where a bracket fits and **Two corner brackets**, which puts one each side along the part it meets. The first connector adds the step at the history marker and the last one taken away deletes it. A fastening whose joint is not there at its step fails, and so does one whose brackets no longer rest on both parts once every part is placed; a joint that goes with a removed part takes its connectors with it. Brackets are drawn as an L of two plates, take part in interference and count, with their bolts and T-nuts, in the parts to buy. The catalog's bracket size, 40 mm legs 36 mm wide, is estimated. Parts that only touch, face to face within the contact tolerance, at the same tilt and with some area in common, make no joint until the `join-parts` operation joins them: the inspector of a profile lists the profiles it touches without a joint, with **Join to** each. The part that meets the other end on goes first, so the joint is the one a bracket fits. A join is a joint like any other: it takes connectors and carries support. It fails when its parts no longer touch, and goes with a removed part.

Slice 7 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/analysis/measure.ts`). The selected profile shows its length in the view as a dimension beside it, standing off along an axis the view can see: above it, in front of it or beside it. It follows the dimensions toggle like the other dimensions, and a span shows the length it takes. **Measure**, above the view, takes two clicks on parts: two parallel faces that look towards each other, at the same tilt and with some area in common across, give the gap between them, measured straight across where they overlap; any other pair of points gives the X, Y and Z offsets from the first to the second, drawn as one leg per axis and named the way the driver sees them (to the left, up, to the front). A third click starts a new measurement, and so does any change to the design or a move of the history marker. While the tool is on, clicks in the view measure instead of selecting; Escape or the button turns it off. Measurements are drawn even with the dimensions hidden, and they are never saved.

## 3. Plates on profiles

Slice 1 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/plates.ts`). A plate rests only on a side face of a profile: it fails when its profile turns so that the face becomes an end, or when it no longer rests on any of its face, and so do a plate on anything but a profile, a growth out of a plate and a span that reaches one. Removing its profile leaves it anchored where it is, facing the same way. Its joint to the profile stays pending, as no corner bracket fits it, until slice 2 brings the bolts that fasten it. The parts to buy group plates by sheet and size, longer side first, whichever way they lie.

Slice 2 was done in November 2026. A fastening holds either corner brackets, each on a side, or a plate's bolts, stored as `{ "typeId": "plate-bolt-m8", "perSlot": 2 }`: as many in each slot of the profile's face as the plate covers with room for a bolt head either side of the slot's center line, spaced evenly along where the two touch. The slots of a face sit one series module apart, as they are drawn. A plate that covers no slot takes no bolts, and its fastening fails when it is moved off them. **Join** lists the profiles a plate touches and the plates a profile touches, and puts the plate first. The inspector of either part sets the bolts in each slot of a plate's joint, with **Two in each slot**; none takes the fastening away. Each bolt is a connector, its box the head on the plate, so it takes part in interference and counts the joint as fastened. The parts to buy count an M8 bolt at the shortest stocked length that goes through the plate and 8 mm into its T-nut, and the T-nut; the head size, that reach and the stocked lengths (12, 16, 20, 25 and 30 mm) are estimated.

## 4. Editing in the view

Slice 0 was done in November 2026. Delete does what the selected step's inspector offers: **Remove this part** for a part, or **Delete this step** for a failed step or one that makes no part; it does nothing in a text field or while a part is about to be added. Slice 1 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/render/hover.ts`, `src/cockpit/editor/hover-view.ts`): the part under the pointer is wrapped in a see-through blue box, apart from the accent of the selection, and its label shows by the pointer after half a second at rest. While a part is about to be added, only the faces of the part it goes on are marked. Slice 2 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/interaction/handles.ts`, `src/cockpit/render/handles.ts`): the end handles of a resize are balls drawn over the view on a stem out of each free end, and the pointer drags them by the point of its ray nearest the profile's axis. A drag resizes the profile's operation as the inspector would, keeping the length within what a profile can have; the change waits as a preview with its value in a box beside the handle, which stays whole within the view. Slice 3 was done in November 2026: every handle is a kind of `Handle` in `src/cockpit/cad/interaction/handles.ts`, which gives the history a drag makes through the inspector's own edits, so a drag can never place a part where the inspector could not. A move arrow changes the start of an anchored part, or how far along and across its parent a hanging one is, in steps of 10 mm of that value; a move that would take a part off its parent stops where it last fitted. An edge handle changes a plate's width or depth and moves its center half as far, so the far edge stays. Slice 4 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/interaction/snapping.ts`): a dragged handle snaps to a place within 10 pixels on screen along its axis, never further than 50 mm, so a handle seen almost end on does not jump from afar. Only parts at the same tilt count, and not the parts that hang from or reach the dragged one, as they move with it; a place the part's operation would refuse is passed over for the next. An end that reaches a part keeps its handle where it was dragged, although a span has no end handle, and the box names the part instead of a value; ✓ or Enter makes the span. After it, a part about to be added got the same handles (`src/cockpit/cad/history/drafts.ts`): the draft is shown at the history marker, and the handles and the reach chosen in its panel edit it there, as they edit a part already added; a drag changes it at once, as the panel already adds it or leaves it out. While a part is placed, it is measured along its profile from each end to its center. A plate's edges then became symmetric by default, as a plate is placed by its center; Shift moves only the dragged edge. A selected plate, or one being placed, shows its width and depth beside its edges, past the handles on them. Slice 5 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/interaction/joint-markers.ts`): each marker sits in the middle of the box the two parts have in common, and its card offers the same actions as the inspector, which it shares; stage 4 is complete.

## 5. Peripherals and adjustments

Slice 1 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/peripherals.ts`). The catalog has a generic bucket seat, a wheel base with its wheel and a pedal set, all estimated. The `mount-peripheral` operation stores the type it was mounted from, its kind, the envelope, mounting pattern, contact points, backrest angle and data source copied from the type, so a design whose type leaves the catalog still opens unchanged; then the part it is mounted on, the face, the end its distance is measured from, the distance and offset to its mounting origin, and `facing`, the way the driver faces when using it, along the face: `"+z"` as usually mounted. It stores an absolute way to face rather than quarter turns, as a plate stores the axis of its depth. A peripheral stands out of any face of a profile, or of either face of a plate, never of a plate's edge; on the end of a profile it is mounted at the center of the end. Its place along a plate is measured on the plate's depth. Its own frame is up out of the face, its front the way it faces and across the third axis, and its envelope and contact points are laid out in it. Off its face it fails, and it goes with the part it is mounted on when that part is removed, as it is bolted through its mounting pattern; a plate whose profile is removed keeps its peripherals. A new one is named after its type, on the middle of a profile's top, or of its front for an upright, or at the center of a plate's outer face, facing the front. It is drawn as simple shapes in its own frame: a seat's cushion and backrest, a wheel base's body with its rim towards the driver, three pedals on a base. **Fit**, in the toolbar of the view, locks the frame: its steps can be selected, but their inspector only says the frame is locked, and they have no handles, joint markers, Delete or **Add a profile**. The toolbar of a selected profile or plate then mounts a seat, a wheel base or pedals, previewed with move handles and snapping, a panel of values and a click on a face; a selected peripheral has its move handles and can be removed. Out of Fit, peripherals are drawn grey and see-through, and clicks and the pointer go through them; selecting a peripheral's step in the history turns Fit on, and turning Fit off unselects one. Fit belongs to the session and is not saved. Interference and support count peripherals by their envelope, as before.

Slice 2 was done in November 2026. The inspector of a mounted peripheral edits the values copied from its type, as values of its step: the width, height and depth of its envelope; a seat's backrest, back from upright, which only a seat has; each hole of its mounting pattern, across it and towards its front on its underside, with its diameter, holes added at its mounting origin as wide as the last one, or for M8 when there are none, and taken out again; and each contact point, across, up and towards its front. Resizing the envelope moves neither holes nor contact points. The panel of a peripheral about to be mounted only places it; its own values wait for its inspector. Its **Type** is swapped in the inspector, as a foot's is: it stays where it is mounted, facing the same way, and takes the values of the new type; a name that was the old type's becomes the new type's, and a name the user gave it stays. A peripheral whose values differ from its type's, or whose type has left the catalog, is marked ✎ edited, and swapping it asks first. Edited values keep the source copied from the type. The backrest angle and the contact points are not drawn until the driver comes in slice 5, and the holes until plates have holes in stage 6.

Slice 3 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/history/adjustments.ts`, `src/cockpit/cad/history/travel.ts`). A profile grown out of a face, a plate or a peripheral stores its adjustment in its step as `{ "axis": "z", "travelMm": 200, "positionMm": 0 }`: an axis of the face it hangs from, the whole travel and the position from its center, towards + on the axis. A span, whose length comes from the part it reaches, and a foot, fastened by its own adapter, have none, nor does a part on the end of a profile, where it has a single place. The design is replayed with every part at the center of its travel, so its joints, connectors, failures and parts to buy never depend on fitting; the fitted pose then moves each part, and what hangs from it, as far as its position says, and the view, the joint markers and interference show that. A corner bracket goes with the profile it fastens, as its T-nut slides in the slot, and a plate's bolts stay in the slots of its profile. A span keeps its length, and the checks list one whose part would have to be longer or shorter to still meet the part it reaches, once any part has an adjustment. A step whose travel takes a profile or a plate off its parent at either end fails, as a part placed off its parent does; a peripheral slides on its mounting, so it only has to stay over part of its parent's face, and a seat on the 40 mm top of a cross member keeps its 200 mm. A seat is mounted with the estimated travel of its rails (`railTravelMm` of its type), along the way it faces; swapping to a type with rails takes their travel and keeps the axis and position, brought within it, and swapping to one without keeps the adjustment. A new adjustment runs the way a peripheral faces, or along the parent of another part, over 100 mm. Moved to another face, an adjustment keeps its axis while it lies on the face, otherwise turns along the parent; a peripheral's turns with the way it faces. In Fit, the inspector of any attached part, the frame's as well, sets the axis, travel and position, adds the adjustment and takes it away; the view's arrow along the travel moves it in steps of 10 mm, kept within the travel, on a bar from end to end with its center marked.

Slice 4 was done in November 2026 (`travelInterferences` in `src/cockpit/cad/history/travel.ts`). Each adjusted part is checked at both ends of its travel, one travel at a time with every other part where its position puts it, and **Parts meeting along a travel** in the design checks lists what it would reach there and at which end, named by the direction of the travel: "Post into Other post: 30 mm front to back, with Post at the front end of its travel". A pair that already interferes as fitted stays in **Parts reaching into each other** and is not listed again, and an end where the part already is adds nothing. Like the spans the adjustments move, the row shows once any part has an adjustment. The view does not mark the pair yet.

Slice 5 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/driver/driver.ts`). The design stores its driver as `{ "statureMm": 1750 }` beside the history, and so does a cockpit file; a design saved before has the 175 cm driver, and a stature outside 140–210 cm is refused. The driver is seated from the contact points where the fitted pose puts them: the hip on the seat, the heels on the pedals and the hands on the wheel, if any, with the torso along the seat's backrest and the average proportions of `anatomy.ts`. The posture is worked out in the plane through the hip along the way the seat faces, so a seat facing back or sideways seats the driver too, and a contact off that plane is taken onto it; a seat facing up or down, or a design without a seat or pedals, seats none and says what to mount. The knee, hip and elbow angles are measured against the estimated GT ranges, and legs or arms that cannot reach are reported. In Fit with nothing selected, the panel is the driver's (`src/cockpit/editor/driver-view.ts`), and the view draws the figure, limbs from joint to joint and a ball on the eye point, over the parts: the seat is drawn from its envelope, with an upright backrest and its hip point inside the cushion, so a figure drawn among the parts would be hidden. The room's monitor arm does not aim at the eye point until the room can include the cockpit design again.

## 6. Plate drawings and holes

Slice 1 was done in November 2026 (`src/cockpit/cad/parts/plate-holes.ts`, `src/cockpit/cad/output/plate-drawing.ts`). A plate's holes are worked out from the design replayed with every part at the center of its travel: a mounting hole for each hole of the mounting pattern of a peripheral mounted on either face of it, named "Bucket seat, hole 2", and a fastening hole for each bolt of its joints to profiles, named "Bolt 1 to Rail", 9 mm across for the M8 plate bolt (`clearanceHoleMm` of its type, ISO 273 medium series, estimated). Nothing is stored and the format is unchanged; the empty own holes of a plate part are gone, and the mounting pattern's types moved to `mounting-pattern.ts`. Plate coordinates are those of its drawing: seen from its face away from the profile, the plate's normal pointing at the viewer, its depth up the page towards + on its axis and the page's right the cross product of up and the normal, so a plate on top with its depth to the front has the driver's right on the right, and the same pattern on a plate under its profile comes out mirrored. In the 3D views each hole is a dark plug through the plate, drawn on the plate as fitted, so it moves with the plate and never with a peripheral sliding on it. **Drawing**, beside 3D, Top and Side, is offered while a plate is selected (`src/cockpit/editor/drawing-panel.ts`) and replaces the view with the plate's SVG, in mm and centered on the plate: its outline, its width above and depth to its right, below it the distance of each column of holes from the left edge and to its left that of each row from the bottom edge, each value once, a circle with a center mark for each hole, "↑ front" (or up, or left) in the top left corner, and a caption with its name, size, sheet and the side it is seen from. Text is a thirtieth of the plate's longer side, between 4 and 12 mm, so it reads at any zoom; slice 4 sets it for print. Pointing at a hole names it with its kind, diameter and distances from the left and bottom edges below the drawing, and a click keeps it. Choosing a camera view, or selecting anything but a plate, goes back to the 3D view. The inspector keeps showing the plate.

Slice 2 was done in November 2026. A peripheral's adjustment stores what it slides on, `"runsOn": "rails"` or `"slots"`, beside its axis, travel and position; a profile's or a plate's has none and refuses it, as they slide on T-nuts in their profile's slot. A seat, whose type has rails, comes on them, and so does a peripheral whose adjustment was saved before, so its plate keeps round holes; a new adjustment of a type without rails runs in slots. Swapping to a type with rails keeps what the old one ran on. The replayed design lists the adjustment of each part by its id (`adjustments`). On a plate, a peripheral in slots turns each of its mounting holes into a slot along the adjustment's axis, centered where the hole was, as wide as the hole and as long as the travel plus the hole; on its rails, or without an adjustment, it keeps round holes. The drawing outlines a slot as two straight sides and two half circles in one closed path, its rows and columns dimensioned at its center, and names it "mounting slot 6.5 mm wide and 106.5 mm long, centered …"; the 3D views draw it as a dark bar between its straight sides with a plug at each end. In Fit, the adjustment of a peripheral on a plate has **Runs on**: its own rails, bolted to the plate, or slots cut in the plate; on a profile there is nothing to choose. The checks of slots, such as a slot crossing another, wait for slice 3.
